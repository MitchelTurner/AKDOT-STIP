Reading Alaska's STIP

$191.7Mfor Ketchikan projects, 2027–2030
$5.63 billionstatewide over four years
Oct 29, 5 p.m.public comment deadline

The Alaska Department of Transportation & Public Facilities (DOT&PF) keeps a four-year list of the road, bridge, ferry and trail projects it plans to pay for with federal money. That list is the Statewide Transportation Improvement Program, or STIP. The draft for 2027–2030 is open for public comment now. This page shows what's in it for Ketchikan and how to weigh in.

It decides what gets federal money

A project that isn't in the STIP can't use federal highway or transit funds.

It's a plan, not a promise

Being listed means DOT&PF expects to move the project forward. Schedules can still slip.

Your comment goes on the record

DOT&PF says it will publish the comments and its responses in Volume 5 of the final STIP.

Report card

How Ketchikan's bridges in the plan are rated

Bridges are inspected under federal standards, and each main part is rated from 0 to 9. If any key part scores 4 or lower, the bridge is rated "Poor." That doesn't mean it's unsafe to drive on. It means it needs significant repair or replacement.

Map #6 · STIP 31719, 34785 · pages V2-152, V2-176

The viaducts are getting patched, but replacing them isn't funded

Viaduct bridge #797 was built in 1955 and is rated Poor (4 out of 9). DOT&PF's project page says the funded work is spot repairs to pavement and structure, plus a new Jim Creek culvert to reduce flooding on Water Street, with construction "as early as 2028." It says these repairs are meant to keep the viaducts safe until they're replaced, and that replacing both viaducts would be separate future projects it is still seeking money for.

In the STIP, a $25 million repair stage for #797 and #997 is scheduled for 2031 or later. A separate #797 replacement project has $10.25 million for design in 2027 and no construction money.

Map #4, #6, #8–10 · FY28 and FY29

A lot of construction lands on Tongass at once

Hoadley Creek Bridge in the West End, the first viaduct stage downtown, and Saxman to Surf Street are all funded for construction in FY28. FY29 adds Deermount to Saxman ($42.5 million), the final viaduct stage and the new Saxman ferry terminal. All of it is on Tongass, the one road that runs through town.

Is there a plan to stagger these jobs, and will major closures avoid cruise season?

Map #8 · STIP 21114 · page V2-154

Deermount to Saxman lists no pavement condition

About $15 million has already been spent on this project, and $42.5 million is set for construction. The project page lists the road's current and expected condition as "Not Available." The work includes rebuilding sidewalks, bike facilities and parking.

What data supports the rebuild, and will the design consider narrower lanes and wider sidewalks?

Map #9 · STIP 35275 · page V2-151

A new ferry terminal at Saxman Seaport, and its price has grown

The draft sets aside $25.8 million, mostly from a federal rural ferry grant, to move the M/V Lituya's Ketchikan landing to Saxman Seaport. DOT&PF says it's carrying out the ferry system's 2045 long-range plan (V6-187). Saxman has wanted a terminal since a 1997 joint resolution with the Metlakatla Indian Community.

Crossing to Annette Bay
Today, from the West End terminal6.7 mi · 45 min

Lituya service in early 2023: 2 round trips a day, 5 days a week. DOT&PF's 2010 site study said a Saxman terminal could allow 7 round trips in a 10-hour day.
Cost of the ferry berth and parking
Jan 2023 engineers' estimate$16.2M
2026 draft STIP$25.8M
The 2023 estimate was for "Concept 3," which used fixed pilings. The ferry system then said it needs a floating berth, which became the preferred "Concept 3A." The report has no cost estimate for 3A.
What would be built, and what people said
  • Plan (Concept 3A): a 140-foot vehicle ramp to a floating berth anchored to bedrock, a 24×36-foot terminal building with a restroom, ticket counter and waiting area, three 250-foot vehicle lanes, about 130 parking spaces, and 290 feet of small-boat moorage. Space is reserved for battery storage for future electric ferries, and the site could serve as a backup berth for the Inter-Island Ferry.
  • Land: Saxman voters rejected selling 7.57 acres to the state in 2007, and the project stalled over lease vs. sale. The current plan leases the land, so it stays with the city. Much of the uplands is leased to Three Bears, which has said it plans to sell fuel from a dock in the harbor.
  • Support: Most people at the January 2023 meetings in Saxman (about 45) and Metlakatla (about 60) supported the move, citing shorter runs and more business for both communities.
  • Concerns: getting from Saxman to the hospital, hotels and stores without a car; cab costs; a waiting room too small for school sports teams; earlier sailings for 8 a.m. work shifts; lower fares. Pennock Island residents rely on the Saxman floats for their safest boat route to town, and one wrote it would "at least triple" their commute.
  • Earlier design money: DOT added a $500,000 design project (STIP 33972) in 2022, and Southeast Conference says the 2024–2027 STIP funded it to reach 35% design. That project number doesn't appear in this draft, and the new project shows $0 spent before 2027.

Is $25.8 million based on the floating-berth design? Will sailings actually increase? Is a shuttle to Ketchikan funded, and what happens to the earlier design work?

Sources: PND Engineers, South Tongass Highway Ferry Terminal Concept Scoping Report for Southeast Conference (July 31, 2023), including Ketchikan Daily News coverage reprinted in its appendix; KRBD, Jan. 2023.

Statewide · pages V3-3, V3-5

The statewide plan counts on more money than DOT&PF expects in the first three years

FY27 through FY29 are over-scheduled by a combined $401 million, offset by room in FY30. When that happens, some projects usually slip to later years. Southeast and the rest of the Southcoast Region get 7% of statewide funding, plus the ferry system.

What makes a comment useful

  • Name the project and its STIP number. Deermount to Saxman is 21114, for example.
  • Say what you want changed: the scope, the schedule, the funding or the design.
  • Explain why in a sentence or two. Firsthand local knowledge counts.
  • One comment per project is easier for DOT&PF to track and answer.

Construction

Building, rebuilding or repairing the road, bridge or terminal.

Utilities

Moving power, water, sewer and phone lines out of the way.

Words you'll see in the draft

Federal fiscal year (FY)
October 1 to September 30. FY27 runs from October 2026 to September 2027.
Advance construction (AC)
The state starts a project with its own money and gets federal reimbursement later.
AC conversion (ACC)
That later reimbursement. An ACC line usually pays for work that has already been done or started.
Parent and child stages
A big project split into pieces. Check the "Project Lifecycle Obligations" table on each page for the full cost.
NHPP
National Highway Performance Program, the federal fund for major highways like Tongass.
State match
The state's share of a federally funded project. For the Ketchikan projects in this draft, it runs from about 9 to 20 percent.
Fiscal constraint
The rule that the plan can't count on more money than is reasonably expected.
